The Idea Behind Vārtā

Born from a Shared Experience

📄

The Paper That Didn't Replicate

During research, we often encounter work that inspires us to explore new directions. Excited and motivated, we spend months setting up experiments, carefully following every protocol described. Sometimes, the results worked as expected, while at other times, they didn’t. Even after repeated trials and carefully following the protocols, they simply didn't.

The reasons could be many, it may be a missing detail or an unstated step, a subtle experimental condition assumed obvious by the authors but invisible to the one following the procedure, or perhaps measurement or analysis errors in the study.

During all this, a researcher spends a lot of time trying testing and tuning the protocol to get the results stated. Sometimes the researcher eventually succeeds and finds the missing piece, but at other times the results simply do not come, and all the attempts go in vain.

Whatever the reason, these discrepancies are almost never discussed openly. The experiment fails, the researcher moves on, and the knowledge — hard-won as it was — disappears.

🤫

The Silence Around Failure

What struck me most was what happened next: nothing. Failed replications don't get published. Negative results rarely make it into journals. We'd hit a wall, quietly abandon the direction, and move on — never knowing if others had hit the same wall.

Meanwhile, somewhere else, another researcher was excitedly reading the same paper, about to spend their next three months on the same dead end. The knowledge existed — it just had no place to share.

💬

What We Actually Needed

A place where you could look up a paper and find not just what it claimed, but what the community had actually experienced. Where a researcher in Nairobi could tell the one in Oslo: "We tried this too — here's what we found, and here's what was missing."

Not a place to attack authors or dismiss work — science is hard and honest mistakes happen — but a space to discuss the merits of ideas, fill in the gaps that papers leave, and collectively build a clearer picture of what actually works.

🌱

The Purpose

Vārtā exists to save research time through community knowledge. If enough people share their experience with a paper — successful replications, partial results, failures, missing details — the community builds a living record that no single paper could contain.

Engage with the work. Ask what's missing. Share what you found. Help the next researcher avoid the months you lost — or confirm the result that changed how you think. That's what vārtā — dialogue — is for.
